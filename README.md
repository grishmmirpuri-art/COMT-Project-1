# COMT Project 1 — Campus Route Planning and Accessibility

**Module:** CIT1C18 — Computational Thinking  
**Institution:** Temasek Polytechnic, School of Informatics & IT  
**Academic year:** 2025/2026 (April semester)  
**Assessment:** Project 1 — Report (30%)  
**Author:** Grishm Chandru Mirpuri

## Overview

This project applies computational thinking to campus navigation at Temasek Polytechnic. It explores how a decision-based flowchart can guide people from a common starting point to one of two destinations, while accounting for accessibility, weather and crowd conditions. The report also compares alternative routes using first-hand observations and walking times.

This repository contains the **project report and its embedded diagrams and photographs**. It is a documentation-based project, not a runnable navigation application.

## Project objectives

- Identify and visually document the start point and two destinations on campus.
- Map routes using recognisable landmarks and step-by-step directions.
- Design a flowchart that branches according to destination, mobility needs, weather and route conditions.
- Evaluate alternative routes for safety, accessibility, shelter and efficiency.

## Locations

| Role | Location |
| --- | --- |
| Starting point | Lift Lobby A, Block 3, Level 3 |
| Destination 1 | Little Skool House |
| Destination 2 | Digital Fabrication & Additive Manufacturing Centre (DFAMC) |

## Method and design

**1. Location documentation:** The report includes photographs of the three locations.

**2. Route planning:** A marked-up campus map identifies the routes considered between the starting point and the destinations.

**3. Flowchart:** The decision-based navigation design offers destination selection and alternative directions. Its branches consider mobility or accessibility needs, rain and conditions such as crowds. The report records **46 flowchart blocks**.

**4. Evaluation:** Multiple routes to DFAMC were examined, including routes via Lobby C/D, the carpark and the Plaza. The discussion addresses wayfinding, poorly lit areas, narrow or obstructed paths, shelter, ramps, stairs and lifts.

## Key findings

For the journey to DFAMC, the report compares these measured walking times:

| Route | Recorded time |
| --- | --- |
| Via the Plaza grassy section | 3 min 50 sec |
| Via Lobby C/D | 4 min 55 sec |

The Plaza grassy-section route was **1 min 5 sec faster** in the recorded comparison. However, the route choice is not based on time alone: the report emphasises alternate paths using ramps, lifts and sheltered walkways for people with accessibility needs or during rainy weather.

These timings reflect the observations documented in the 2025 project; they should not be treated as live journey estimates or verified accessibility instructions.

## Computational thinking concepts

- **Decomposition:** Breaking navigation into smaller actions and decisions.
- **Algorithm design:** Expressing route instructions as a sequence of actions and branches.
- **Conditional logic:** Choosing routes based on user needs and environmental conditions.
- **Evaluation:** Comparing possible solutions using practical considerations and measured walking times.

## Project contents

The report is organised into three assessed tasks:

1. **Location Selection and Visual Documentation** — photographs of the locations.
2. **Flowchart Design** — campus map and decision-based route instructions.
3. **Route Justification and Evaluation** — comparison of paths and explanation of selected routes.
